The form frame
Issue #207. Every form in Vitals is the same three parts: an optional header, one scrolling body, and an action footer that never scrolls. The footer rides the keyboard. Nothing is broken today — Save is reachable on both platforms — so this is priority 1, the feel: with the keyboard up, the thing you are about to press is already under your thumb, and you never scroll to find it.
1 · The ruling
Six sentences. Everything below is detail under them.
Pinned, not scrolling. The footer is a sibling of the scroll view, never its last child. It is flex: none; the body is flex: 1 with minHeight: 0. The body is the only part that ever shrinks.
One avoider per presentation. Keyboard avoidance belongs to the outermost frame on screen and there is exactly one of it. On a pushed screen that is the form; inside a sheet it is the sheet, and the form inside adds none.
The footer owns the bottom inset; the body owns none. formFooterPaddingBottom(keyboardVisible, insets.bottom) is the whole rule, and it lives in tokens.ts so no form can get it half right.
The footer rides the keyboard’s own curve. Vitals adds no duration, no spring and no offset to that travel — the only thing that would do is desync the footer from the keys.
The footer never collapses. Two rows with the keyboard up, always: the preview strip and the action row. A note row is keyboard-down only. On the smallest phone the body gets small; the footer does not move.
No new native module. KeyboardAvoidingView from React Native core, as PortionSheet already uses. Charts and forms both have to run in Expo Go.
2 · The inset, and who owns it
The one mistake this pattern exists to prevent: adding the safe-area inset on top of the keyboard. The keyboard is already sitting on the home indicator, so the inset would lift the button off a band of dead keys. formFooterPaddingBottom in src/theme/tokens.ts is the only place that decision is made.

3 · Tokens
Four distances and one duration. Nothing else in this pattern is a number a form may type. Colour needs nothing new: a footer is, by ruling, the ground it floats over — bg.surface in a sheet, bg.canvas on a pushed screen — and its divider is line.hairline at StyleSheet.hairlineWidth, like every other divider in the app. A form only asks design-lead for a colour when it wants a ground of its own.

4 · Sheets — one avoider, and only one
PortionSheet already wraps its scrim and sheet in a KeyboardAvoidingView (issue #94). That is the precedent and it stands: in a sheet the avoider is the sheet’s, because the scrim has to rise with it. A second avoider inside the form would shift the content twice.

5 · Mechanism — what ui-engineer builds
One component, owned by ui-engineer, so the second and third form need no design input at all. Suggested home: src/components/form/FormFrame.tsx. Names below are the contract; the internals are ui-engineer’s.
Shape: → (the body) + (the footer). Footer outside the scroll view, always.
behavior: 'padding' on iOS. On Android too — Expo SDK 57 is edge-to-edge, so the window no longer resizes itself under the keyboard. This choice lives in the frame, in one line, for every form: if the carried device check shows a double shift on some Android build, it changes there and nowhere else.
keyboardVerticalOffset: the height of whatever chrome sits above the frame — the stack header on a pushed screen, 0 in a sheet. A prop with a 0 default, never a literal inside the frame.
Kill automaticallyAdjustKeyboardInsets on the body. It exists in FoodForm today to make Save scrollable; with a pinned footer it double-shifts the content. Keep keyboardShouldPersistTaps="handled" — a chip or Save with the keyboard up must land on the first tap (issue #79).
Never useAnimatedKeyboard, react-native-keyboard-controller, or a hand-rolled Keyboard.addListener height animation. One mechanism, in core, that Expo Go runs.
Props: footerBg (the host’s ground), avoidsKeyboard (default true; false when a sheet already avoids), headerOffset (default 0), footer (the rows), children (the body), testID.
Gutter: the frame owns the side gutter (layout.gutter) on both body and footer, so the divider runs edge to edge. The host screen must stop adding paddingHorizontal around the form — /foods/new and /foods/[id] both do today.
testIDs: ‹id›-frame, ‹id›-body, ‹id›-footer, ‹id›-footer-divider. The existing ‹id›-save and ‹id›-cancel keep their names inside the footer.
Tests: useSafeAreaInsets() needs a provider in RNTL — wrap in with a fixed inset rather than mocking the module, so the 34 pt and the 0 pt cases are both real. Assert Save is rendered outside the scroll view, which is the acceptance line “reachable with the keyboard up without scrolling”.
Thumb: the primary action stays in the bottom third in every state on these boards, and Cancel keeps the narrower half. Both clear size.button.primaryHit = 52 pt, over the 44 pt floor.
6 · The divider
One hairline, on the footer, and nothing at the top of the body. It says “there is more form under here”, so it follows the bottom of the scroll, not the top.
Shown while the body can scroll and is not at its end: contentOffset.y + layoutMeasurement.height < contentSize.height - 1. Hidden when the body fits, or is scrolled to the bottom — there is nothing under the footer to hint at.
Fades over 120 ms (motion.events.footerDividerFade). Opacity on a 1 pt line, so reduce motion keeps it as is (reduced.kind: 'same') — there is nothing vestibular in a hairline.
Colour line.hairline at StyleSheet.hairlineWidth, the same divider the log rows and the search sheet draw. foodForm.footerDivider already resolves to it.
7 · Reduce motion
Motion is feedback, never decoration — so there is very little of it here, and what there is survives.
The footer’s travel is the keyboard’s. The OS animates the keyboard and honours the user’s own settings; KeyboardAvoidingView follows it frame for frame. Vitals owns no duration for it, which is why motion.events gains no footerLift entry — a token nothing consumes is issue #210’s mistake, and one that fought the OS curve would be worse.
The divider fades either way: 120 ms of opacity is under the 150 ms where a cross-fade starts to read as movement.
Nothing else moves. No sheet re-height, no content re-flow, no scroll animation on focus. If a form wants to bring a field into view on focus, that is the scroll view’s own behaviour, not an animation this pattern owns.
8 · The small screen, where the footer and the keyboard compete
Drawn on the “Small screen” board: 375 × 667, no safe-area inset, a 260 pt keyboard. The footer is 126 pt and does not move; the body takes what is left and scrolls. The note row being keyboard-down only is what keeps the footer at two rows on every phone.
